Licences and regulatory information

Moving other people's money is one of the most heavily regulated activities there is. This page explains what that involves — and states plainly that this project holds none of it.

Regulatory status of this project Nabha Remit holds no licence, authorisation or registration of any kind, in any country. It is a static website built as a front-end portfolio exercise. It cannot accept, hold, transmit or convert money, it has no customers, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by any real financial institution. Nothing on this site should be read as an offer of financial services.

Why licensing exists

When you hand money to a transfer provider, there is a gap — sometimes minutes, sometimes days — during which your money is neither in your account nor your recipient's. It sits with the provider. Licensing exists to govern that gap: to make sure the money is held separately from the company's own funds, that it cannot be used to fund the business, and that it is returnable if the company fails.

The second purpose is harder to see but just as important. Transfer networks are attractive to people laundering the proceeds of crime, financing terrorism or evading sanctions. Licensed providers are required to verify who their customers are, monitor transactions for suspicious patterns, and report what they find. This is the reason identity checks feel disproportionate to the amount you are sending — the obligation attaches to the activity, not the sum.

What a provider typically needs

Authorisation is granted country by country, and a provider operating in thirty markets may hold dozens of separate permissions. Common requirements include:

  • Authorisation as a payment institution or money transmitter from the financial regulator in each jurisdiction where it operates, often with a separate licence per state or province.
  • Minimum capital held permanently, scaled to transaction volume, so the business can absorb losses without touching customer money.
  • Safeguarding arrangements that keep customer funds in segregated accounts at regulated banks, or covered by an insurance policy or bank guarantee.
  • Anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing programmes with a named officer personally accountable to the regulator.
  • Fit-and-proper assessments of directors and significant shareholders.
  • Regular reporting on volumes, safeguarding balances, complaints and suspicious activity.
  • Data protection registration where required, and compliance with the applicable privacy regime.

How to check a real provider

This is genuinely useful knowledge, and it applies to any service you are considering trusting with money:

  1. Find the licence or registration number, which should appear in the website footer or the legal pages.
  2. Go to the regulator's own public register directly — type the address yourself rather than following a link from the provider's site.
  3. Search the register for the number and confirm the company name matches exactly, that the permission covers money transmission, and that the status is active.
  4. Check whether customer funds are described as safeguarded or segregated, and with which institution.

A provider that does not publish a licence number, or whose number does not appear on the regulator's register, should not be trusted with a transfer regardless of how polished its website looks. Most national regulators also publish a warning list of unauthorised firms, which is worth checking.

About this project

Nabha Remit was built to practise front-end development: layout, responsive design, accessible components and interaction patterns, written in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no frameworks or dependencies. The brand name, logo, copy, sample data and all artwork were created for the project. Any resemblance to an existing company's structure reflects the fact that payments websites solve similar problems, not any connection between them.

If you want to see how the demonstration behaves, the transfer calculator and currency converter are the interactive parts. Both run entirely in your browser on hard-coded sample rates.
