Understanding rates

What the mid-market rate means

Currencies trade in a two-sided market: one price to buy, a slightly higher price to sell. The midpoint between those two is the mid-market rate — the number you see on a search engine or a financial news site.

No consumer provider gives you that exact number, because converting currency costs them something. What varies enormously is how much they add on top. A transparent provider tells you the margin; a less transparent one quotes "our rate" and lets you work it out yourself.

  • Mid-market rate — the market midpoint, no margin added
  • Offered rate — what you actually convert at, margin included
  • Spread or margin — the gap between the two, expressed as a percentage
  • Inverse rate — the same relationship read the other way round

Spotting the margin

Say the mid-market rate is 1 INR = 0.01131 USD and a provider offers you 0.01098.

Mid-market0.011310
Offered0.010980
Difference0.000330
Margin2.92%

On ₹1,00,000 that margin is worth about ₹2,920 — far more than any headline fee. Divide the difference by the mid-market rate to get the percentage.
