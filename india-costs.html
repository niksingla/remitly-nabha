What this would cost to run from India

A site like this one is the cheap part. The expensive part is the licence behind it. Here is every approval a real cross-border transfer business needs in India, what each charge comes to, and a rough total.

Step 1

Approvals you must hold

There is no single "remittance licence" in India. Which one you need depends on whether money flows in, out, or both.

Step 3

Tax you pay and tax you collect

GST

18% on your margin

Charged on the service value, not the amount transferred. Under CGST Rule 32(2) you either tax the spread against the RBI reference rate, or use the slab method — 1% up to ₹1 lakh, then tapering, with taxable value capped at ₹60,000. You pick one method for the whole financial year.

TCS

You collect it, free of charge

Nil up to ₹10 lakh per PAN per year, then 20% for general purposes and 2% for education or medical. The threshold is cumulative across every authorised dealer, so you must track a limit you cannot see — and withholding 20% at checkout costs you conversions.

Corporate

22% – 30% on profits

Plus surcharge and cess. The concessional regime lands around 25% all-in. Relevant only once you are profitable, which in this business is rarely year one.

Which is why almost nobody starts at the bottom of this table. The usual path is to launch on top of a licensed partner, prove the volume, then apply for your own authorisation once the locked capital is justified. Approval itself takes nine to eighteen months and is not guaranteed — the advisory fees are spent either way.
