1. What this site actually does with your data Nothing. Remitly Nabha is a static demonstration site with no backend. Every form on it is intercepted by JavaScript and shows a notice instead of submitting. There are no analytics scripts, no tracking pixels, no cookies set by this site, no third-party embeds and no network requests of any kind beyond loading the page's own stylesheet and scripts. The sections below describe what a genuine payments provider would need to collect, and exist to demonstrate how a privacy notice is written.

2. What a real provider would collect

A licensed money transfer service cannot operate anonymously — identity verification is a legal requirement, not a product choice. In practice it would collect:

  • Identity information — your name, date of birth, nationality, residential address, and images of an identity document together with a selfie used to match against it.
  • Contact information — email address and mobile number, used for verification codes and transfer notifications.
  • Financial information — your payment method details, your transaction history, and the account or wallet details of people you send to.
  • Recipient information — the name and payout details of each recipient, which you provide on their behalf.
  • Technical information — device identifiers, IP address and approximate location, used for fraud detection rather than advertising.
  • Support interactions — records of chats, calls and emails with the support team.

3. Why it would be needed

Each category would map to a specific purpose and a legal basis:

PurposeData usedBasis
Executing your transferIdentity, financial, recipientPerformance of a contract
Verifying who you areIdentity documentsLegal obligation
Preventing fraudTechnical, transaction patternsLegal obligation and legitimate interests
Providing supportContact, support recordsPerformance of a contract
Product improvementAggregated usage dataLegitimate interests
MarketingContact informationConsent, withdrawable at any time

Note the asymmetry worth looking for in any real notice: identity and transaction data sit under contract and legal obligation, which means you cannot opt out and still use the service. Marketing sits under consent, which means you can.

4. Who it would be shared with

A provider would share data with payout partners and receiving banks in the destination country, with identity verification and fraud screening providers, with payment processors handling your card or bank payment, and with regulators and law enforcement where legally compelled.

A trustworthy notice is explicit that customer data is not sold to advertisers or data brokers, and names the categories of processor rather than hiding behind "trusted partners".

5. How long it would be kept

Financial regulation typically requires transaction and identity records to be retained for five to seven years after an account closes, depending on the jurisdiction. That obligation outlasts any deletion request, which is why a privacy notice should say so plainly rather than implying everything can be erased on demand.

Data held only on the basis of consent — marketing preferences, for example — would be deleted as soon as consent was withdrawn.

6. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you would generally have the right to:

  • Ask for a copy of the personal data held about you
  • Have inaccurate information corrected
  • Request deletion, subject to the retention obligations above
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interests
  • Withdraw consent for marketing at any time
  • Receive your data in a portable, machine-readable format
  • Complain to your national data protection authority

A real provider would commit to responding within one month and would not charge for a first request.

7. Cookies

This demonstration site sets no cookies and uses no local storage. A production service would typically use strictly necessary cookies for session management and security, and would require explicit opt-in before setting anything used for analytics or advertising.

8. International data transfers

Cross-border payments inherently involve cross-border data. Sending money to another country means transmitting recipient details to an institution in that country. A real notice would explain the safeguards used when data moves between jurisdictions, such as standard contractual clauses or an adequacy decision.

9. Contact

A genuine provider would name a data protection officer and publish a dedicated address for privacy requests. For this project, the placeholder address privacy@remitly-nabha.example appears in that role and is not monitored.
