Pricing

How a transfer is priced

Three things add up to the total you pay. We show each one separately rather than rolling them into a single figure.

1

The exchange rate

We start from the mid-market rate — the midpoint between buy and sell prices on the open market — and apply a small margin. That margin is between 0.25% and 0.9% depending on how fast you want the money to arrive.

2

The transfer fee

A flat fee on Express transfers, waived above a threshold. Economy transfers carry no fee at all because the money travels on slower, cheaper rails.

3

Your payment method

Bank transfers and UPI are free to use. Card payments carry a surcharge because the card networks charge us to accept them.

Comparing

The real cost is rarely the fee

A zero-fee transfer with a 3% rate margin costs more than a ₹199 transfer at 0.4%. When you compare providers, work out the amount the recipient ends up with — that is the only number that matters.

  • Ask what rate is being applied, not just the fee
  • Check the mid-market rate independently before you send
  • Watch for "our rate" wording — that means a margin is baked in
  • Confirm whether the recipient's bank deducts anything on arrival

Pricing questions

Currency markets move continuously during trading hours. A quote reflects the market at the moment you asked, which is why it is locked for a short window once you start a transfer.

Many providers run a promotional rate on a first transfer. In this demo we show that as a one-off zero-fee first send, with the standard margin applied afterwards.

The fee stays the same, but weekend rate margins are typically slightly wider because markets are closed and the rate has to cover Monday's opening movement.

The delivery promise described on this site refunds the transfer fee automatically when a transfer misses its stated window for reasons within the provider's control.
