1. Important notice — read this first Nabha Remit is a fictional brand and this website is a static front-end demonstration built as a learning exercise. It is not a licensed money transmitter or payment institution in any jurisdiction, it cannot hold or move funds, and it is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by any real payments company. No agreement is formed by reading or interacting with these pages, and no service is offered. The text below is written in the style of a genuine user agreement purely to demonstrate how such a page is structured.

2. Scope of this agreement

In a real deployment, this agreement would govern the relationship between the provider and any person who opens an account, together with the privacy notice and any product-specific terms covering cards, business accounts or API access. It would set out what the provider undertakes to do, what the customer undertakes to do, and what happens when something goes wrong.

Where a product-specific term conflicted with this general agreement, the product-specific term would take precedence for that product only.

3. Eligibility

A genuine provider would require that you are at least eighteen years old, that you are resident in a country where the service is licensed to operate, that you act on your own behalf rather than for an undisclosed third party, and that you are not subject to financial sanctions.

It would also reserve the right to decline an application, or to close an account, where those conditions are not met or cannot be verified.

4. Your account

You would be responsible for keeping your credentials confidential, for the accuracy of the information you provide, and for notifying the provider promptly if you suspected unauthorised access. The provider would be responsible for maintaining the account, for safeguarding funds held in it, and for providing a record of transactions.

Identity verification would be required before the first transfer, and again at intervals or when activity changed materially. Where verification could not be completed, the account would be restricted rather than silently failing.

5. Transfers

A transfer instruction would be treated as given when you confirm it, at which point the quoted exchange rate is locked for a stated window. The provider would undertake to deliver within the window shown at confirmation, subject to clearing of your payment, completion of any verification, and the operating hours of the receiving institution.

You would be responsible for the accuracy of recipient details. Where a transfer failed because details were wrong, funds would be returned, although a bank may apply its own charge for a returned payment.

6. Rates and fees

All charges would be disclosed before you confirm a transfer: the transfer fee, any payment method surcharge, the exchange rate being applied, and the precise amount the recipient would receive. The provider would commit to not deducting anything from the recipient's amount after confirmation.

Every rate, fee and delivery estimate shown anywhere on this demonstration site is illustrative sample data generated in the browser.

7. Cancellation and refunds

You would be able to cancel any transfer that had not yet been collected or credited, with funds returned to the original payment method. Refund timing would depend on that method — instant to an account balance, several business days back through a card network.

Cash transfers left uncollected would be refunded automatically after a stated period, typically thirty days.

8. Prohibited use

A real agreement would prohibit using the service for anything unlawful; for gambling where restricted; for transactions involving sanctioned persons, entities or territories; for funds derived from criminal activity; on behalf of an undisclosed third party; or to circumvent limits by splitting transfers across accounts.

It would reserve the right to refuse, delay or reverse a transaction, and to report activity to the relevant authorities where required by law.

9. Liability

The provider would accept responsibility for losses caused by its own failure to execute a properly authorised instruction, and would not accept responsibility for losses arising from incorrect details you supplied, from a payment you authorised after being deceived by a third party, or from circumstances beyond its reasonable control.

Nothing in such an agreement would exclude liability that cannot lawfully be excluded, including liability for fraud.

10. Complaints

A complaints process would include a written acknowledgement within two business days, a substantive response within fifteen, and clear information about escalating to the relevant financial ombudsman or regulator if you remained dissatisfied.

11. Changes to this agreement

Material changes would be notified at least thirty days in advance, with the right to close your account free of charge before they took effect. Changes required by law or that were purely favourable to customers could take effect sooner.

12. Governing law

A real agreement would specify the governing law and the courts with jurisdiction, usually determined by your country of residence, and would preserve any mandatory consumer protections available to you locally regardless of that choice.
